Guideline 4.3 rejection: Spam

Guideline 4.3 is one of the harder rejections to reverse. Apple thinks your app is a duplicate of other apps, or a thin entry in a crowded category. Fixing it usually means changing what the app is, not a line of code.

Apple decides. This page is our own reading of the App Review Guidelines as of September 2026, not Apple's wording, and following it does not guarantee approval. The current text at developer.apple.com is the authority.

What Apple's message says

Apple now splits this guideline in two. Paraphrased:

  • 4.3(a): the app shares a binary, code or concept with other apps, often ones from the same developer, with only the name, colors or content changed.
  • 4.3(b): the app is in a category Apple considers saturated (for example simple flashlight, fortune-telling, fart or dating clones) and does not offer a unique, high-quality experience.

The message rarely says which other apps it compared yours to. It may say the app "duplicates the content and functionality" of others, or that the category already has enough apps like it.

Why AI-built apps hit it

AI builders make it fast to generate a working app from a prompt, and that speed invites patterns Apple flags:

  • Several apps from one template, each for a different city, client or niche, with the name swapped.
  • An idea many other people also generated in an afternoon (a habit tracker, a quote app, a simple chatbot shell) with no clear difference.
  • A resubmitted copy of an app that was rejected before, under a new name or account.
  • A template for a business (restaurant, salon, gym) published by the developer rather than the business, which also touches 4.2.6.

How to fix it, step by step

  1. Read the message for (a) or (b). Duplicate apps and saturated categories need different answers.
  2. For duplicates (4.3(a)), combine the variants into one app with in-app choices (location, client, theme). Remove the extra apps or stop submitting them.
  3. For saturated categories (4.3(b)), write down, in one sentence each, what your app does that others in the category do not. If you cannot, the app probably needs a real difference before resubmitting.
  4. Make the difference visible in the first minute of use and in the first two screenshots, not only in the description.
  5. If a business owns the content, submit from that business's own developer account.
  6. Consider the goal. If the aim is reach rather than an App Store listing, a web app or a progressive web app may serve it better, with no review.
  7. If you believe the reviewer is wrong, reply with concrete, checkable differences, and consider the App Review Board appeal.

What to reply in the Resolution Center

Fill in the brackets and delete anything you did not do. Only list differences a reviewer can see and verify. Adjectives such as "better" or "more intuitive" do not help.

Hello,

Thank you. Regarding Guideline 4.3: this app is not a copy of another app. It differs from others in its category in these specific ways:

- [Verifiable difference 1, with where to see it]
- [Verifiable difference 2]

We do not publish other versions of this app. [If you consolidated: "We have combined our previous variants into this single app and removed (names) from sale."]

Best regards,
[Your name]

Questions

Can a Guideline 4.3 spam rejection be reversed?

Sometimes, but it is one of the harder ones. It usually takes a real change to the app, such as merging duplicate apps into one or adding a clear difference, plus a reply that lists verifiable differences. Apple decides.

Is my app rejected for 4.3 because it was made with AI?

Not by itself. 4.3 is about apps that duplicate others or add nothing new to a crowded category. AI builders make near-identical apps quick to produce, which is why they often meet this guideline.

Should I resubmit the same app under a new account?

No. Resubmitting a rejected app under another name or account is itself a pattern Apple treats as spam, and it can put the developer account at risk.
