Guidelines 4.0 and 5.6: design, copycats and developer conduct

These rejections are less about a missing feature and more about how the app looks and who it appears to come from. 4.0 is a design quality finding, 4.1 is about imitating another app or brand, and 5.6 is Apple's code of conduct for developers.

Apple decides. This page is our own reading of the App Review Guidelines as of September 2026, not Apple's wording, and following it does not guarantee approval. The current text at developer.apple.com is the authority.

What Apple's message says

Paraphrased:

  • 4.0 Design: parts of the interface are hard to use on the device. Common examples: content cropped or cut off, text too small to read, layouts that do not fit iPad, or a sign-in that sends the user out to Safari to finish.
  • 4.1 Copycats: the app's name, icon, look or content imitates a popular app or brand, or suggests a link with a company that it does not have.
  • 5.6 Developer Code of Conduct: Apple expects honest dealings. The sub-parts cover manipulating ratings and reviews (5.6.1), accurate developer identity (5.6.2), fraud in discovery such as fake installs (5.6.3), and apps with sustained quality problems or complaints (5.6.4).

Why AI-built apps hit it

AI builders make a polished first version quickly, and some of that polish is borrowed:

  • The prompt asked for "an app like [famous app]", and the result copied its colors, layout and even its name pattern.
  • The icon is a generated image that closely resembles a well-known logo.
  • The desktop web layout is squeezed onto a phone, so buttons sit under the notch or the home indicator, and text is cut off.
  • The app shows a "Rate us 5 stars to unlock" prompt, or a custom rating dialog that sends only happy users to the App Store (5.6.1).
  • The seller name on the developer account does not match the business or brand named in the app (5.6.2).

How to fix it, step by step

  1. Read which number Apple cited. The fixes for 4.0, 4.1 and 5.6 are different; fix only what was named.
  2. For 4.0, test every screen on a small and a large iPhone (and iPad, if supported). Respect the safe areas (env(safe-area-inset-*) in CSS), set a readable base font size, and keep sign-in inside the app.
  3. For 4.1, make the app clearly your own: your own name, icon, colors and wording. Remove other companies' names and logos unless you have written permission, and say so in review notes if you do.
  4. For 5.6.1, use the system rating prompt (SKStoreReviewController, or a Capacitor plugin for it). Do not reward, gate or filter ratings.
  5. For 5.6.2, make identities match. The seller name, the support page and the company named in the app should agree. If you build for a client, the client's own developer account is usually the right place to publish.
  6. For 5.6.4, look at the reviews and crash reports. Fix the problems users describe before submitting again.
  7. Upload a new build if anything inside the app changed, and update screenshots to match.

Questions

What does a Guideline 4.0 Design rejection usually mean?

Something on screen is hard to use on the device: cropped content, text too small, a layout that does not fit iPad, or sign-in that leaves the app for Safari. The rejection usually includes a screenshot; fix that screen and check the others for the same issue.

Can my app look like a popular app?

Using common patterns such as a tab bar is fine. Copying another app's name, icon, colors and layout closely enough to be confused with it is a 4.1 problem. Make the branding your own.

Can I ask users for a 5-star rating?

Use Apple's system rating prompt. Offering rewards for ratings, or showing the App Store rating request only to users who said they are happy, goes against 5.6.1.
