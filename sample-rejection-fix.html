1. The rejection, decoded

Apple's message mixes boilerplate with the part that matters. Here is the rejection as received (shortened), then what each part means.

Cited Guideline 2.1, App Completeness

"We were unable to sign in with the demo account"

We were unable to sign in with the demo account credentials you provided. An error message was displayed: "Invalid login." Additionally, the app displayed a blank screen after we tapped "Continue" on iPad Air (5th generation) running iPadOS.

What it means. Two separate problems. The reviewer could not get past sign-in, so they did not see the rest of the app. Then, on iPad, the screen after sign-in stayed white.

What we found. The demo account in App Review Information had been created on a test database that the iOS build no longer uses, so the password was correct but the account did not exist. The blank screen came from the web layout: on iPad widths, the plan screen waited for a sidebar that only loads on desktop, and never rendered.

Guide: 2.1 app completeness

Cited Guideline 5.1.1(ii), Data Collection

"The purpose string does not sufficiently explain the use"

Your app's code references one or more APIs that access sensitive user data, and the app's permission request does not clearly and completely describe how the app will use the data. NSCameraUsageDescription: "This app needs camera access."

What it means. The text in the camera permission popup is too generic. Apple wants a specific reason tied to a feature the user can see.

What we found. The camera is used only to add a photo to a recipe. The photo library key was also generic, and would likely have been the next point.

Guide: 5.1.1 data collection and purpose strings

Cited Guideline 5.1.1(v), Account Deletion

"The app supports account creation but does not include an option to initiate account deletion"

Apps that support account creation must also offer account deletion within the app. Emailing support or visiting a website is not sufficient if the process is not fully in the app.

What it means. Users who can sign up in the app must also be able to delete their account in the app. A "contact us" line does not count.

What we found. Settings had Log out only. The backend had no function to delete a user's plans, shopping lists and photos.

Guide: 5.1.1(v) account deletion

What we did not change

  • Prices, subscriptions, agreements, tax or banking. None were part of this rejection, and we do not touch them.
  • Your app's features, design or text beyond what the points above required.
  • Your users' data. The delete function was tested only on test accounts we created.

Thank you for the review. We have addressed each point in build 1.0 (5):

Guideline 2.1
- The demo account in App Review Information has been replaced and
  tested on a fresh install. It includes a sample meal plan.
- The blank screen after "Continue" on iPad has been fixed. We tested
  on iPad Air (5th generation) and 13-inch iPad, in portrait and landscape.

Guideline 5.1.1(ii)
- The camera and photo library permission texts now explain the
  specific use: adding a photo of a dish to a recipe.

Guideline 5.1.1(v)
- Users can delete their account in the app: Settings > Account >
  Delete account. This removes the account and its meal plans,
  shopping lists and photos.

Please let us know if anything else is needed.

Best regards

You can change anything in this text. If you would rather post it yourself, copy it as is.
